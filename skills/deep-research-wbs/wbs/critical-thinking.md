# Curiosity and critical thinking (Week 2 lecture)

Contents: definitions, why it matters, the claim-evidence-reasoning model, the five plain questions, worked exercises, habits, how Claude coaches this.

## Definitions the lecturers use

- **Curiosity**: "a form of cognitively induced deprivation that arises from the perception of a gap in knowledge or understanding" (Loewenstein, 1994). Plain version: the desire to fill a gap between what you know and what you want to understand.
- **Critical thinking**: "Active, persistent, and careful consideration of any belief or supposed form of knowledge in the light of the grounds that support it, and the further conclusions to which it tends, constitutes reflective thought" (Dewey, 1910, p. 6).

## The model: claim, evidence, reasoning (plus limits)

| Step | Their argument | Your argument |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | What are they arguing? | What are you arguing? |
| Evidence | What supports their argument? | What supports yours? |
| Reasoning | Why does that evidence support their claim? | Why does that evidence support yours? |
| Limits | What objections or limits apply? | What objections or limits apply? |

Use `wbs/templates/claim-evidence-reasoning-worksheet.md`.

## The five plain questions

1. What exactly is being claimed?
2. What evidence supports it?
3. What else could explain it?
4. What information is missing?
5. What would change my mind?

## Worked exercises from the lecture (use for practice)

**"Company profit rose by 20% this year." Good news, bad news, or more information?**
Strong answers ask for the comparison base (last year, peers, forecast), the absolute amount, revenue against profit, one-off gains or asset sales, accounting policy changes, cost cuts that borrow from the future, cash against profit, inflation, margin, who makes the claim and what they gain from it.

**Campus cafe trial evening.** Students asked for later opening. One Tuesday the cafe stayed open until 8 p.m.; sales from 5 to 8 p.m. were 180 pounds; two staff worked the extra hours; the manager calls it a success.
The lecturer's defensible recommendation: the trial looks promising, one evening is thin evidence, so check the additional costs and changes in demand, then repeat the trial on different nights. Useful questions: wages and running costs for those hours, whether the sales moved from earlier hours, whether a Tuesday is typical, novelty effect, what "success" means, what threshold would justify the change.

**Audit scenario (Week 8).** Revenue jumped 12% on the last day of the reporting period. Answer A: "Ratios look fine, materiality not breached." Answer B: "Why is revenue up 12% on the last day? Show me delivery notes. What changed internally? Is this cut-off manipulation?" Answer B shows expertise because it probes cause, evidence and manipulation risk.

**Native question test (Week 8).** A client sends a costing spreadsheet with odd numbers. Options: A "Has this supplier changed their batch size or invoicing template recently? Last quarter their decimals shifted and caused the same issue" (native, built on lived specifics); B "Could you clarify the data source and how it aligns with the costing method?" (book learner); C "Please review the data for accuracy and ensure consistency with internal controls" (generic, LLM-like). Aim for questions anchored in a named document, a date or a past pattern.

## Habits to build (Week 2 slide)

- In a lecture: write down one question about a claim.
- When reading: identify the claim and its evidence.
- When writing an assignment: test one alternative explanation.
- In teamwork: ask what information is missing.

## How Claude coaches this

- Give the student a claim from their reading and ask for the five questions in their own words, one question at a time. Respond to their answers with the next gap you see.
- Ask "what would change your mind?" at the end of any argument the student builds.
- For a statistic, ask where it comes from, who produced it, what it compares to, and whether the student can reach the original.
- Keep the practice short, around five minutes, and pair it with a live item from the student's current reading.
