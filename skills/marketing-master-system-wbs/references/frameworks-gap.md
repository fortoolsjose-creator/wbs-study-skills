# Frameworks the installed skills lack

Each section says what the framework claims, how to use it, and which questions test it against a case. Claims marked (verified) were read on a publisher or index page on 2026-11-22. Claims marked (to confirm) come from general knowledge and need a page check before they go into assessed work. Full references with verification levels are in `sources.md`.

## 1. The marketing mix: 4Ps and 7Ps

**What it says.**
- Borden (1964) introduced the marketing mix and described the marketing executive as a "mixer of ingredients" who builds a mix of procedures and policies to produce a profitable enterprise (verified).
- McCarthy's four Ps (product, price, place, promotion) are the common simplification (to confirm in the primary source).
- Booms and Bitner (1981) extended the mix for services with people, process and physical evidence (verified through an index entry for the chapter).

**How to use it.** Treat the Ps as a checklist for completeness at the plan stage (phase 4). Pair each P with the skill that covers it: product and price with `pricing`, place with `revops` and `launch`, promotion with `content-strategy` and `ads`, people, process and physical evidence with `biz-customer-journey`.

**Questions that test it.**
- Which P does the evidence in this case support, and which is assumed?
- Does the case sell a service, so that the extra three Ps matter?
- Do the Ps fit together, or does one (for example a premium price) contradict another (for example discount promotion)?

## 2. How brands grow (Sharp, 2010)

**What it says (verified through publisher-linked summaries and the book's listing).**
- Brands grow mainly by winning more buyers.
- The two market-based assets are mental availability (the brand comes to mind in a buying situation) and physical availability (the brand is easy to find and buy).
- Distinctive assets stay consistent over time.

**How to use it.** At phase 2 and phase 5, check reach, distribution and memorability before loyalty programmes. Use it as a counterpoint to `grad-brand-equity`, which centres on brand knowledge and loyalty.

**Questions that test it.**
- What data in the case supports penetration over loyalty as the growth route?
- Does the category resemble the repeat-purchase categories the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute studied, or a different one (to confirm in the book)?
- How would mental availability be measured here, and by whom?

## 3. Short and long term: Binet and Field (2013)

**What it says (verified from the report's executive summary).**
- The IPA Databank suggests an average optimum split of about 60:40 between brand-building and activation spend, and the split varies by category.
- Rational, response-focused campaigns give stronger short-term sales effects and do not deliver maximum long-term success.
- Volume growth comes quickly, and pricing effects take longer. A short-term focus does not maximise long-run profit.
- Brands need metrics for both horizons.

**How to use it.** At phase 4 (budget) and phase 6 (measurement), test whether the plan weights only quick activation. Pair with `mmm-modeling` and `brand-measurement`.

**Questions that test it.**
- How were the cases in the databank selected, and what does that do to the 60:40 average (to confirm in the report's method section)?
- Does the 60:40 average apply to this category and budget size?
- Which metric in the plan tracks the long term, and over what window?

## 4. Governance checklist (practitioner list, no academic source)

Use this list at phase 7 when the plan needs an operating routine. It is not citable in assessed work.
- Quarterly review of goals, spend and results against the plan.
- Agency or vendor scorecard: deliverables, cost, response time, results.
- Claims review before publication: every performance, price and environmental claim has evidence on file.
- Crisis communication owner, approvers and holding statement.
- Board report: three results, three risks, one decision needed.

## 5. Evaluation prompts for the framework skills

| Framework | Skill | Prompts |
|---|---|---|
| SWOT | `biz-swot` | Are the items prioritised and evidenced, or only listed? Who judged them? Does the TOWS step produce choices the firm can act on? |
| STP | `biz-stp` | Is each segment measurable, accessible, substantial and different in response? What data defines the segments? |
| BCG | `biz-bcg-matrix` | Is relative market share a good proxy for profitability in this industry? What does the matrix ignore (synergies, brand, capabilities)? |
| Ansoff | `ansoff-matrix` | Which route has evidence, and which is a guess? What does diversification assume about capabilities? |
| Blue Ocean | `blue-ocean-strategy` | Is the claimed new space truly uncontested, and what evidence shows non-customers would buy? |
| Brand equity | `grad-brand-equity` | Are awareness, associations and loyalty measured, or inferred? Whose perception counts? |
| Customer journey | `biz-customer-journey` | Is the journey built from data or from assumption? Which touchpoints does the firm control? |
| Conjoint | `conjoint-diagnostics` | Do stated choices match real purchases? Are the attribute levels realistic? |
| Elasticity | `algo-price-elasticity` | Does the price change come from an experiment or from history with other changes mixed in? |
| MMM | `mmm-modeling` | How long is the data, and do channels move together so that effects cannot be separated? |
