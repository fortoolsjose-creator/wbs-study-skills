# What WBS expects: module map, assessment pipeline, participation

Contents: module snapshot, delivery, reconstructed calendar, rules of the game, participation, integrity and support, confirm before relying.

## Module snapshot

- Module: IB1450 "Core: Foundations, Skills and Debates" (Week 2 slides print the code as IB145). Warwick Business School (WBS), University of Warwick, first-year undergraduate. Cohort mixes Business and Accounting and Finance students.
- Stated aims (business-overview lecture): develop essential study skills; develop skills around AI; introduce the areas of management (marketing, operations, accounting and finance, HR, management and leadership).
- Debate theme: the Week 2 and Week 8 decks both end on the controversy "Do digital technologies (including AI-driven analytics, automated decision-support systems and algorithmic assessment tools) enhance or diminish professional judgement?" The Week 2 deck labels this the "assignment question" in a slop example from the 2025 cohort, so the individual assignment very likely revolves around it. Confirm in the current brief.

## How the module is delivered

- Weekly lecture with live polling on Vevox (log in and stay logged in; engagement earns participation marks) and a Teams chat.
- Directed learning: weekly videos with a quiz at the end. The quiz answers let staff see who engaged. The final directed-learning week came right after the business-overview lecture; after that the same skills are applied in workshops.
- Workshops (two hours for some groups, check the timetable; some groups moved because of clashes). They are the engine of the group project.
- Seminar in Week 4 on a past paper (Week 2 deck, "Coming next").
- Group project (workshops Weeks 5 to 8) and an individual assignment (Weeks 9 and 10 give more on academic writing).
- Week 3 lecture was led by a guest lecturer (Michael Mortenson per the Week 2 deck); its content is not in the supplied material.
- Lecture series after the business overview: marketing, operations, accounting and finance, then HR, management and leadership; one session on the assignment; a week 10 integration lecture. Week 8 (Dr Ruowen Xu) covers digital technology and professional judgement.

## Rules of the game

- Attendance at the workshops is part of the group assignment. Missing two workshops without a good reason excludes the student from the group assignment. Good reasons go through mitigating circumstances.
- Evidence comes from academic literature and reports. Personal opinion, lecturer opinion and chat-room opinion carry no weight in the report.
- Definitions use the student's own words and avoid recycling the term inside its definition (lecturer's hint during the quiz discussion).
- Disagreement needs a reason ("If you disagree, you've got to say why").
- Every claim, number or chart used in coursework carries a traceable source. The Week 2 deck shows a chart whose source line reads "?????" as a warning.

## Participation (10% of the module mark)

Components named in the lecture: directed learning (videos and end quizzes), attendance, participation in lectures (Vevox and chat), and workshop materials submitted on time.

How it is judged: staff collect data across the term and place students into broad groups (fully engaged, partly engaged, not engaged enough), then grade. One miss does not matter much. Attendance data had gaps this term, so nobody is penalised on flawed data alone. A student who misses a directed-learning week should say so to staff and keep up the pattern afterwards.

Use `wbs/templates/participation-tracker.md` to keep a weekly record.

## Confirm before relying

Ask the student for these, one at a time, and write the answers into the working notes:

1. The full group brief (sector options, the three named example companies, word limit, format).
2. The individual assignment brief and question for this year.
3. Marking criteria and weightings for group and individual work.
4. Referencing style (the slides use author-date, for example "Abbott (1988)").
5. The AI permission statement and declaration form for each assessment.
6. Exact deadlines and the student's workshop day.
7. How group contributions are assessed (peer assessment, contribution statements).
8. The Week 2 videos on finding and evaluating sources and the library resources linked from the workshop page. They are the module's own method for source work and were not supplied. Claude's source pipeline (`wbs/source-dossier-workflow.md`) applies in the meantime.
