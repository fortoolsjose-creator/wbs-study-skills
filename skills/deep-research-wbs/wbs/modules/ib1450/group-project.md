# Group project: the sustainability report for the board

Contents: what the brief says, pipeline, content scaffold, team process, how Claude helps.

## What the brief says (from the lecture; the full brief on MyWBS has several pages, read it)

- The team picks one of three sectors and plays an anonymous, fictitious company in it. The brief names example companies in the other sectors so the team can locate its main competitors.
- Trigger: some main competitors announce sustainable business practices.
- Deliverable: a report for the board. Board members hold a mix of opinions and starting points and the report must address them.
- Standard: evidence-based analysis that leads to a recommendation. Evidence comes from academic literature and reports.
- Teams of five or six, assigned through the green button on the MyWBS module page.
- Workshops are integral. Missing two without a good reason excludes the student from the group assignment.

The sector names, word limit and marking criteria are not in the supplied material. Ask the student.

Each deadline lands at 9:00 AM on the day of the group's next workshop. Repeat the same sequence for later assignments.

## Content scaffold

### Sustainability definition and pillars (business-overview lecture)

- Definition from the Brundtland report (nearly 40 years old): meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
- Three pillars: environmental protection (human activity stays within the biological limits and carrying capacity of the Earth); social equity (development improves quality of life for all people and fosters social solidarity); economic prosperity (economic activity supports social and environmental health and avoids destroying it).

### Framing question for the team

Are the pillars trade-offs (more environmental protection means less economic prosperity), win-wins (doing one raises the others) or a paradox (no obvious way to promote all three, so the firm navigates and adopts a stance)? A classmate posted: "Surely it is almost impossible for a business to remain as profitable as it was if it focuses on environmental sustainability." The lecturers called this the point the group will explore and said the answer must come from evidence in the academic literature and reports. Treat that statement as a hypothesis, then look for evidence that supports it, evidence that undermines it, and conditions under which each applies (sector, time horizon, regulation, customer base, cost of capital).

### Concepts from the overview lecture that feed the report

- Purpose of business: Oxford definition (trade and commercial activity, no mention of profit or shareholders); Drucker (meet the customer's need first, then profit); Friedman (shareholder value); stakeholders (owners, employees, customers, shareholders, suppliers, society). See `wbs/modules/ib1450/business-foundations.md`.
- Short-run profit versus long-run position: Amazon's early losses, Ryanair softening treatment of customers.
- External environment through PESTLE (political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental).
- Lobbying: large businesses lobby for their own interests, which sometimes means supporting regulation that smaller rivals struggle to meet. Same pattern appears with AI labs and regulation. One finding, several possible explanations.
- Business functions as lenses on the recommendation: operations (supply chain, processes), marketing (demand, brand), accounting and finance (cost, financing, disclosure), management, leadership and HR (people, capability, culture).

### Mapping the board

List the board's plausible starting positions (for example shareholder-value first, stakeholder-first, risk-averse, follow-the-competitor) and show for each finding which position it supports and challenges. This suggestion fits the brief's mention of mixed views; confirm with the brief.

## Team process (suggestions)

- Roles: coordinator, source lead, evidence-tracker, editor, devil's advocate (rotating), AI-log keeper. Rotate across stages.
- Shared source table with status (found, read, summarised, verified).
- Decision log with date, decision, reason, who agreed.
- Each member records their own AI use; the team agrees rules in the Team AI map and states them in the declaration.
- Weekly check: attendance at workshops, deadlines, each member's contribution.

## How Claude helps a group

- Run a meeting agenda and capture decisions on request.
- Ask each member the five plain questions about the team's draft claims.
- Check whether the executive summary matches the body.
- Run the source search and verification for the team (`wbs/source-dossier-workflow.md`) and share one dossier.
- Deliver structure, counterarguments, critique, literature synthesis and full draft sections, the executive summary and the final report on request.
