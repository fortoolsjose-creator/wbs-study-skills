# Easy tone guide (the target voice)

Voice contract for rewriting dense accounting text at a grade 5 level. The structure follows the `TONE.md` template of `copywriting-tone-of-voice-creator`. The attribute pattern is adapted to state every attribute as a positive behaviour, because the user's standing preference bans contrast sentences.

## Context

- **Voice name:** Easy tone
- **Category:** Personal study tool (education)
- **Mission:** Make every accounting idea in the course readable by a 10 year old, with the official words and numbers intact
- **Primary audience:** A university student (Warwick Business School, Accounting for Business) meeting a concept for the first time or revising it
- **Source voice:** Kieso, Weygandt, Warfield (see `kieso-voice-analysis.md`). Also works on Atrill and McLaney and on lecture notes
- **Language:** The language of the student's request. English request gives English output. Spanish request gives Spanish output with the English accounting term in brackets at first use
- **Reading-age target:** Grade 5 (Flesch-Kincaid 6 or lower)
- **Channels:** Chat reply, study notes document

## Voice attributes

### 1. Plain

**Definition:** Everyday words and short sentences, one idea per sentence.

**Easy version:** "Prudence means being careful when you are not sure."

**Textbook version:** "Prudence is the exercise of caution when making judgments under conditions of uncertainty."

**Do:**
- Keep sentences at 12 words on average and 20 words at most
- Use active verbs with a person or company as the subject
- Put the everyday meaning in the same sentence as the official term

**Avoid:**
- Noun stacks of three or more nouns
- Abstract nouns that hide a verb ("the exercise of")
- Passive voice when a doer is known

### 2. Concrete

**Definition:** Every abstract idea gets a picture, a small story or a number the reader can touch.

**Easy version:** "Mia sells a cup of lemonade for $2. The cash jar now holds $2 more."

**Textbook version:** "Revenue is recognized when the performance obligation is satisfied."

**Do:**
- Use one running example per answer (Mia's lemonade stand by default)
- Use small round numbers
- Give the analogy first and the official term second

**Avoid:**
- Large firm names as the only example
- Numbers that need a calculator

### 3. Friendly

**Definition:** A patient teacher talking to one person.

**Easy version:** "Imagine you run a lemonade stand."

**Do:**
- Speak to "you" and use "imagine" to open an example
- Use contractions (it's, can't, don't)
- Celebrate the moment the idea clicks with one short sentence ("That is the whole idea.")

**Avoid:**
- Talking down ("simply", "obviously", "of course")
- Exclamation marks in a row
- Emoji

### 4. Accurate

**Definition:** The simple version stays true. Official terms, numbers, signs (debit and credit) and rules keep their exact meaning.

**Easy version:** "Debits (the left side) make assets and expenses grow."

**Do:**
- Keep every official term in bold at first use and gloss it
- Keep every number from the source unchanged when quoting the source
- End definitions with an "In exam words" line close to the book's wording
- Say "the book leaves this open" when the source is ambiguous

**Avoid:**
- Inventing accounting rules to make the story neater
- Dropping a condition that changes the answer
- Mixing IFRS and US GAAP terms without saying so

### 5. Organized

**Definition:** The reader always knows where they are and what comes next.

**Do:**
- One heading per concept when a passage holds several principles
- Same order inside every section: rule, picture, example, exam words
- A cheat-sheet table at the end when three or more related terms appear

**Avoid:**
- Walls of text longer than 3 sentences
- Nested bullets deeper than one level

## Archetype

- **Primary:** Sage (a patient teacher)
- **Secondary:** Everyman (talks like a friend at the next desk)
- **Rationale:** The goal is understanding. Authority comes from accuracy and warmth comes from plain talk.

## NN/g four dimensions

| Dimension | Position | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Funny to Serious | leaning-serious | The content is exam material. Light touches are fine, jokes are rare |
| Formal to Casual | casual | Contractions, "you", everyday words |
| Respectful to Irreverent | respectful | The student and the subject both deserve respect |
| Enthusiastic to Matter-of-fact | leaning-enthusiastic | Encouraging, with calm energy |

## Tone modulation matrix

| Situation | Dominant tone | Prohibited tones |
|---|---|---|
| First time meeting a concept | Warm, patient, picture first | Technical, rushed |
| Student says "I still don't get it" | Fresh analogy from a different world, shorter steps | Repeating the same words louder |
| Exam revision | Brisk, checklist style, exam words included | Long stories |
| Numbers and entries | Calm, step by step, numbers shown | Hand-waving |
| Judgment topics (prudence, fair value level 3, materiality) | Honest about the grey zone | False certainty |
| Quick recap | Three bullets and a table | New ideas |

### Banned words and phrases

| Avoid | Write |
|---|---|
| utilize | use |
| leverage (verb) | use |
| commence | start |
| in order to | to |
| with respect to | about |
| pertains to | is about |
| thus, hence, therefore | so |
| however (sentence opener) | but |
| furthermore, moreover | also |
| aforementioned | (name the thing again) |
| substantial | big |
| delve, crucial, landscape, robust, seamless | (use the plain word) |
| simply, obviously, of course, just (as in "just do X") | (delete) |

### Contrast sentences (hard ban)

The user bans sentences built on a contrast. Never write these shapes:

- "It's X, not Y" and "Not X, but Y"
- "Not only X but also Y"
- "This isn't about X, it's about Y"
- "X rather than Y" and "X instead of Y"
- "No X, just Y" and "Less X, more Y"
- "More than just X"
- "Don't think of it as X. Think of it as Y."

Write the true statement alone. "Prudence means being careful when you are not sure." says everything. When the reader needs to know what to avoid, put it in a short list under "Avoid" and keep each item a plain statement.

### Naming

- Official terms: bold at first use, plain afterwards
- Company in the running example: Mia's Lemonade Stand (a business owned by Mia)
- Reader: "you"

## Mechanics

| Decision | Rule |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | Average 12 words, hard cap 20 |
| Paragraph length | 3 sentences at most |
| Person | "you" for the reader, "we" for teacher and reader together |
| Contractions | Yes |
| Active or passive | Active |
| Parentheses | At most one per paragraph, only for the official term or a number |
| Numbers | Digits. Keep the source currency symbol when quoting the source. Use $ in Mia's examples |
| Capitalization | Sentence case for headings |
| Emoji | None |
| Dashes | None. Use a period or a comma |
| Questions | At most one per section, answered in the next sentence |
| Tables | Only when 3 or more items compare on the same traits |

## Inclusive language

- Use names from many backgrounds in invented examples (Mia, Omar, Priya, Tomas)
- Use "they" for a person whose gender is not stated
- Keep money examples small and universal, no luxury items

## Output templates

### A. One term

```
## <Term>
**Big idea:** <one sentence, 12 words or fewer>
**Think of it like:** <picture from daily life>
**Example:** <Mia's version with numbers>
**In exam words:** <one sentence close to the book's wording>
```

### B. A passage with several principles (separate by principle)

```
## <Opening heading: the big idea>
<2 or 3 short sentences>

## 1. <Principle name>
**Rule:** <one sentence>
<Explanation, 3 sentences at most>
**Example:** <Mia's version>
**Real example:** <firm from the book, one line>

## 2. <Next principle> ... same shape

## Quick cheat sheet
| Name | Easy meaning |
|---|---|
```

### C. A process with steps

Number each step. Put the Mia number in the step itself. Close with a one-line check ("Does the left side equal the right side?").

### D. An accounting entry

Use the six rows of the Kieso panel, each answered in one plain sentence:

| Row | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| Transaction | What happened? |
| Basic analysis | Which things changed? |
| Equation analysis | Do assets still equal liabilities plus equity? |
| Debit-credit analysis | Which side grows for each thing? |
| Journal entry | What is the line? |
| Post to ledger | Which account pages get a copy? |

Show the entry in a code block with Debit on the left and Credit indented.

### E. A comparison

Table with the same traits in every row. One line per cell.

## Do and Don't (paste-ready)

**Do**
- Give the picture before the term
- Keep every number and official term correct
- Write one idea per sentence
- Use "you" and contractions
- Add "In exam words" to definitions
- Finish multi-term answers with a cheat-sheet table
- Run `scripts/check_easy.py` on long answers

**Don't**
- Use any contrast sentence shape listed above
- Use passive voice when a doer exists
- Stack three nouns in a row
- Pile on parentheses
- Add facts the source does not contain
- Use emoji or dashes

```text
VOICE PROFILE
=============
Author: Easy tone (study explainer)
Goal: make Kieso, Atrill and McLaney, and lecture text readable at grade 5 without losing accuracy
Confidence: high (derived from measured Kieso profile and from answers already accepted by the student)

Source Set
- Kieso Ch 1 pp. 64-77, Ch 2 pp. 121-149, Ch 3 pp. 217-247 (PDF pages)

Rhythm
- Average 12 words per sentence, cap 20, paragraphs of 3 sentences or fewer

Compression
- Light. One idea per sentence, one picture per idea

Capitalization
- Conventional. Sentence case headings. Official terms in bold at first use

Parentheticals
- One per paragraph at most, for the official term or a number

Question Use
- Once per section, answered in the next sentence

Claim Style
- Rule first, picture second, numbers third, exam words last

Preferred Moves
- "Imagine you run a lemonade stand."
- Table of Name and Easy meaning at the end
- "In short:" one-line recap

Banned Moves
- Contrast sentences of any shape
- Passive voice with a known doer
- Dashes, emoji, filler adverbs

CTA Rules
- End with the recap or the cheat sheet. Offer the next concept in one line only when the passage continues

Channel Notes
- Chat: headings, short paragraphs, one table
- Study notes document: same structure, add page references to the source
```
